# AI Will Collapse Organizational Complexity

One of the least discussed effects of AI is how much organizational complexity it will eliminate.

Companies didn't become large only because there was a lot of work to do.

They became large because coordinating the work was expensive.

You needed a product manager to translate customer requirements.

An architect to figure out the system.

Engineering managers to break the work down.

Developers to implement it.

QA to verify it.

Project managers to make sure everyone was moving.

And then meetings, Jira, Slack, documentation, and processes to keep all these people synchronized.

AI doesn't merely make each one of these people more productive.

It starts collapsing the interfaces between these roles.

A requirement can become a technical plan. The technical plan can become tickets. Tickets can become code. Code can trigger tests. Failed tests can trigger another implementation cycle.

All without information constantly moving between humans.

We often discuss AI as a productivity multiplier.

I think the bigger change will be organizational compression.

Companies that once required 100 people might eventually operate with 20.

Not because the other 80 jobs were pointless.

Because much of the coordination that required those 80 people can now happen in software.
