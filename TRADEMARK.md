# Trademark and brand policy

The LFG source code is released under the [MIT License](LICENSE). The MIT grant
covers **copyright in the code**. It does not, and by its terms cannot, grant
rights in our **trademarks** — the name "LFG", the LFG logo, and our other brand
assets. Those are reserved.

This page explains, in plain terms, what that means for you.

## What the MIT license already gives you — no permission needed

You may do all of this freely, commercially, and without telling us:

- Run LFG for yourself, your team, or your company, on any infrastructure.
- Run it for your own clients as part of your internal delivery process.
- Read, modify, and fork the code.
- Redistribute it, including modified versions, under the MIT terms
  (keep the copyright notice and license text).
- Charge money for work you do with it.

We do not gate any of the above, and there is no usage ceiling, seat count, or
call-home. If you never contact us, everything above still applies.

## What the trademark reserves

You need our written permission to:

- Use the name "LFG" or the LFG logo as the name or branding of a product or
  service you offer to others.
- Imply that your fork, distribution, or hosted service is official, endorsed,
  certified, or supported by LFG Inc.
- Register a domain, app-store listing, company name, or social account that
  is likely to be confused with LFG.

Distributing a **rebranded** build under your *own* name is not a trademark
problem — that is the normal way to do it. Remove our marks, use yours, and you
are within both the MIT license and this policy.

## Nominative use — always fine

You never need permission to refer to us accurately:

- "Built on LFG", "Powered by LFG", "compatible with LFG"
- Comparisons, reviews, tutorials, conference talks, blog posts
- Naming LFG in documentation, changelogs, or dependency lists

Plain, factual references are fine. Please don't restyle our logo, use it as
your own product icon, or present the reference as an endorsement.

## White-label

If you want to deliver LFG to your own customers under your own brand, we offer
a **white-label arrangement**. It is available on request, and it is about
support rather than permission — the MIT license already lets you rebrand a
fork yourself.

What the arrangement adds:

- Branded builds and a managed instance we operate for you
- A written trademark permission, if you want to reference LFG explicitly
- Release and upgrade support, and a support channel with our team
- Commercial terms in writing, which most procurement teams require

Request one at **<hello@lfg.run>** or via <https://lfg.run/white-label/>.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether a particular use is covered, ask us at
**<hello@lfg.run>**. We would rather answer a short email than have you guess.

This policy may be updated; changes are not retroactive for uses already
permitted in writing.
